# Real-time Event-camera Stereo Visual Odometry via Keytime Gaussian Process Regression 

Nikan Nobari 1 and Jonathan D. Gammell 1

Abstract — Event cameras have microsecond-level temporal resolution and high dynamic range which make them more resilient to motion blur and poor illumination than standard frame-based cameras. Event-camera visual odometry (VO) pipelines maximize these benefits when they process the asynchronous event stream at the native temporal resolution. Continuous-time Gaussian process (GP) regression and a white-noise-on-acceleration (WNOA) prior can handle asynchronous measurements but result in a prohibitively large estimation state when applied naively. This paper presents a continuous-time event-camera stereo VO pipeline that maintains the native measurement times of asynchronous events while also running in real time. It reduces the estimation states to keytimes while maintaining full temporal resolution by interpolating measurements to their exact timestamps with a physically founded WNOA prior. This decouples the state size from the dense number of measurements without discarding their asynchronous nature. The real-time continuous-time VO pipeline is evaluated on the MVSEC and DSEC datasets. It provides estimates in real time that are more accurate than ES-PTAM, a state-of-the-art discrete estimator, in all but one of the tested sequences. The pipeline respectively provides estimates at 22 Hz and 6 Hz on MVSEC and DSEC and RMS relative errors of 46 · 10 −2 cm and 3.8 · 10 −2 degrees across all valid sequences, which were 

11 and 15 times better than ES-PTAM, respectively. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Frame-based cameras capture visual information as discrete images. Visual odometry (VO) and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) pipelines built on these cameras have achieved remarkable success [1]–[3] but they struggle in the presence of motion blur or poor image contrast which degrade feature tracking and cause inaccurate estimates. This often occurs with highly dynamic camera motions and in environments with poor illumination. Event cameras use a fundamentally different sensing paradigm that can avoid these problems. They report pixel intensity changes with microsecond-level temporal resolution and possess a higher dynamic range than traditional frame-based sensors. This combination reduces the limitations of standard cameras and promises VO pipelines that are more resilient to motion blur and poor lighting [4]. Event-camera VO pipelines must account for the asyn-chronous nature of event data to achieve the full potential of event cameras. Some discrete event-camera VO pipelines maintain the asynchronous measurement times by using motion-compensated event frames [5]–[10] or extracting event-based features [7], [11] in their front end but then group events during state estimation and reduce temporal resolution.

Continuous-time estimation methods alternatively provide tools to handle the asynchronous measurement times during estimation [12]. Some Gaussian process (GP) continuous-time VO pipelines (e.g., GPCT [13]) use a white-noise-on-acceleration (WNOA) motion prior [14] to maintain the individual measurement times but naive application of the estimator requires a state for each event feature and does not run in real time. This paper presents a real-time continuous-time event-camera VO pipeline that uses keytime interpolation [14]–[17] to reduce the size of the estimation state. The pipeline considers event features at individual timestamps to reject outliers with motion-compensated RANSAC (MC-RANSAC) [18]. It then defines a smaller estimation state at a selected set of sparse keytimes but still evaluates each measurement at its individual measurement time by interpolating between the keytimes with a WNOA prior (Fig. 1). This ensures that the pipeline runs in real-time while maintaining the temporal resolution of the event camera. The presented pipeline is evaluated on the multi-vehicle stereo event camera (MVSEC) [19] and driving scenarios for event cameras (DSEC) [20] datasets. The experimental results show that the keytime approach maintains similar estimation accuracy as the not-real-time GPCT pipeline [13] while signifi-cantly reducing state size in all tested sequences (Sec. IV-C). It also achieves better estimation accuracy than event stereo par-allel tracking and mapping (ES-PTAM) [21], a state-of-the-art discrete stereo pipeline, in all but one of the tested sequences (Sec. IV-D). It does this with an estimation frequency of 22 Hz on MVSEC and 6 Hz on DSEC by using keytimes and a arXiv:2610.02601v1 [cs.RO] 1 Oct 2026 producer-consumer architecture analogous to keyframing [5]. The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section II provides a brief review of event-camera VO pipelines. Section III details the real-time continuous-time pipeline. Section IV presents experimental results of the pipeline across two datasets and comparisons against GPCT and ES-PTAM on the MVSEC and DSEC datasets. Finally, Section V summarizes our findings and concludes the paper. II. R ELATED WORK 

Event-camera VO pipelines can generally be categorized by how they process the asynchronous event stream. Discrete-time approaches accumulate events into representations that lose the native temporal resolution of the sensor (Sec. II-A). Continuous-time approaches accommodate the asynchronous nature of event cameras and maintain their full temporal fidelity (Sec. II-B). 

A. Discrete-time Approaches 

An intuitive approach for processing event streams is to accumulate events into discrete frames or equivalent spatial representations. These formulations leverage the extensive lit-erature on direct or indirect methods for frame-based cameras. Early event-camera VO works estimate rotation-only 

SO (3) poses [22] or SE (3) trajectories by incorporating RGB-D sensors [23]. Kim et al. [24] presents an event-only pipeline with three decoupled filters that additionally provides depth and intensity estimates but requires a GPU to achieve real-time performance. Monocular event cameras are frequently paired with inertial measurement units (IMUs) in visual-inertial odometry (VIO) pipelines. Many of these systems fully discretize the event data [25], [26] but some of them address the asynchronous events with a combination of motion-compensated event frames and frame-based cameras [5], [6], [8]–[10], [27]. These improve feature tracking by creating more stable features but do not preserve the individual event timestamps during estimation. Zuo et al. [28] demonstrates the tracking capabilities of a monocular event camera under challenging conditions but requires a prior semidense 3D map. Chamorro et al. [29] uses line-based features and achieves real-time updates with an error-state Kalman filter but the success of the pipeline is dependent on a sufficient number of straight-line features. Shiba et al. [30] utilizes contrast maximization to jointly estimate optical flow, depth, and egomotion but the linear motion assumption of optical flow can break with longer sequences and the pipeline does not run in real time. Event visual odometry (EVO) [31] is a direct parallel tracking and mapping (PTAM) [3] approach. ESVO [32], El Moudni et al. [33], and ES-PTAM [21] follow a PTAM approach for stereo vision. These pipelines do not address asynchronous events in the back end and lose the temporal resolution of event cameras. Hadviger et al. [11] presents an indirect stereo VO pipeline that uses only event cameras. It extracts features from the event stream using Arc* [34], which maintains individual event times, but this temporal fidelity is discarded by assuming all features in a frame occur at the same time during outlier rejection and state estimation. ESVIO [7] is an indirect stereo VIO pipeline that uses a modified version of Arc* on motion-compensated time surfaces, both of which address the asynchronous events in the front end but the back end does not use this temporal resolution. Liu et al. [35] and ESVO2 [36] extend ESVO with an IMU, which helps create a smoother trajectory, but also do not preserve the individual timing of events in the back end. All of these discrete time approaches are ultimately limited by grouping the continuous stream of events into fixed inter-vals, which approximates the native temporal resolution to discrete times. This reduction in temporal resolution can rein-troduce motion blur and cause inaccuracies in state estimation. 

B. Continuous-time Approaches 

Continuous-time formulations are a natural framework for processing asynchronous measurements from event cameras. These methodologies were initially popularized for scanning LiDARs [14] and rolling shutter cameras [37]. A review of continuous-time trajectory estimation is available in [38]. Mueggler et al. [12] presents a continuous-time monocular event-camera VO that models the SE (3) trajectory with cubic B-splines but it requires an a priori map for localization. This work was extended into a VIO pipeline [39] that removes the need for an a priori map, but the usage of numerical Jacobians instead of analytical Jacobians hinders real-time performance. Liu et al. [40] presents a monocular VO pipeline where the continuous trajectory is modeled as a GP with a WNOA prior and a sparser set of states, similar to our formulation. The estimation state is updated with each new event, which couples state estimation with outlier rejection and may introduce different sources of errors than the standard VO approach of rejecting outliers before estimation. The evaluations are also limited to five-second sequences. Wang et al. [41] uses cubic B-splines for trajectory represen-tation and present a batch volumetric contrast-maximization approach. They map scenes in 3D but restrict the estimated egomotion to planar motion. Liu et al. [42] also uses cubic B-splines to model the trajectory and achieves real-time VIO but requires frame-based cameras for feature initialization. Lu et al. [43] introduces a direct stereo visual-inertial ve-locimeter using cubic B-splines. The incremental velocity up-dates lack a bundle adjustment which makes it more suscepti-ble to drift compared to batch or sliding-window VO pipelines. IDOL [44] is a batch, line-based VIO pipeline. It uses Gaussian preintegrated measurements and associates events with them to handle their asynchronous nature but the batch structure of the pipeline and the time-consuming line-feature extraction reduces real-time performance. Wang et al. [45] introduces an event-camera monocular VIO pipeline with local GPs on SO (3) and R3 and white-noise-on-jerk (WNOJ) priors [46]. It does not achieve real-time performance and separate optimization of SO (3) and 

R3 is generally less accurate. GPCT [13] is a stereo pipeline that represents the trajectory as a GP with a WNOA prior. It addresses asynchronous events by using MC-RANSAC [18] for outlier rejection and placing a state at each measurement time, but this causes large state sizes that limit real-time performance. Elms et al. [47] present a continuous-time SLAM pipeline designed for rapidly spinning objects that parameterizes the trajectory with an orbital motion model rather than B-splines or GPs. Guo et al. [48] is a rotational SLAM pipeline driven by contrast maximization that can use both linear and cubic splines. Subsequent work focuses solely on the bundle adjustment back end and replaces the contrast maximization with a photometric approach using linear splines [49]. All these pipelines are only presented on SO (3) .This paper presents a stereo event-camera-only VO pipeline that achieves real-time estimation rates while maintaining the asynchronous nature of event cameras. In contrast to discrete-time methods, it uses the individual measurement times of each event feature, which maintains the advantages of event cameras. In contrast to existing continuous-time methods that use B-splines, it uses a GP representation with a WNOA prior which provides a more physically founded model. In contrast to other GP methods, it both uses MC-RANSAC to isolate out-lier rejection from state estimation, and uses keytime interpo-lation to decouple the state size from the number of measure-ments. It reduces optimization times significantly and achieves real-time performance with a producer-consumer architecture. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

This paper presents a continuous-time event-camera stereo VO pipeline that preserves the asynchronous event data’s temporal resolution while also achieving real-time estimation rates. This is accomplished by estimating a sparser set of pose and velocity states at designated keytimes and implementing a producer-consumer structure. The continuous-time formula-tion allows the pipeline to evaluate reprojection residuals at the individual measurement times via GP interpolation while enforcing trajectory smoothness via a WNOA motion prior. The pipeline takes asynchronous event-features with in-dividual timestamps from any event feature extractor e.g., [13], [34], [50]. It uses motion compensated RANSAC (MC-RANSAC) to separate outlier features using a constant-velocity model. It then selects keytimes to represent the continuous trajectory as a GP. This trajectory is estimated by minimizing the reprojection residuals of each measurement at its individual time by interpolating between keytimes using a WNOA prior. The estimated continuous camera trajectory can be queried via interpolation at any time both a posteriori 

and online [14]–[17].

B. Outlier Rejection 

Frame-based VO pipelines use Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC) [52] to remove outlier measurements. RANSAC estimates a discrete transform between two frames by assum-ing that the measurements in each frame occur simultaneously. This assumption does not hold for asynchronous event cam-eras since every feature observation has a unique timestamp. MC-RANSAC [18] instead estimates a constant velocity considering the individual timestamps of each measurement. The closed-form variant of MC-RANSAC defines the residual in Euclidean space instead of nonlinear measurement space. This variant is approximate but faster to compute than the exact, iterative method using the measurement space residual [18].

The real-time pipeline is evaluated on the MVSEC [19] and DSEC [20] datasets, which have sensor resolutions of 

346 × 260 and 640 × 480 , respectively. The first experiment compares the keytime estimator to the baseline continuous-time approach, GPCT [13]. This evaluates the effect of adding keytimes on the accuracy of the trajectory estimated using a continuous-time GP formulation. The second experiment com-pares the keytime estimator to the publicly available results of the discrete event-based ES-PTAM [21] which reports real-time performance on MVSEC. This evaluates the accuracy of the keytime estimator as a real-time estimation pipeline. All experiments were conducted on a computer with an Intel ®

Core ™i7-9750H CPU at 2.60 GHz and 16 GB of RAM. 

A. Implementation Details and Parameters 

The pipeline is implemented in C++ using the GTSAM optimization library [54]. Custom factor types were developed to implement the WNOA motion prior and the keytime-interpolated measurement models. The pipeline utilizes an incremental batch optimization approach via iSAM2 [55]. The baseline GPCT method is also implemented in C++ using GTSAM, sharing the WNOA factor implementation but utilizing discrete stereo measurement factors. The pipeline is demonstrated using appearance-based features on accumulated event clusters [13], but is capable of processing asynchronous measurements from any feature extractor, e.g., [34], [50]. Event clusters are generated by accumulating 10 , 500 events per cluster for MVSEC and 

150 , 000 events per cluster for the higher-resolution DSEC. Asynchronous measurements were accumulated sequen-tially until a new keytime was created and then processed. A new keytime state was created at the last event in each cluster. The new keytime state was initialized by extrapolating the previous keytime state using the local constant-velocity model. Features are independently matched between the current cluster and the preceding five clusters in a frame-by-frame basis using LIBVISO2 [56]. Feature extraction is performed across both full-resolution and half-resolution clusters. An initial half-resolution downsampling is applied to accelerate feature extraction on DSEC. Outlier rejection is performed using MC-RANSAC with an inlier threshold of αthresh = 0 .1, a target confidence of 

0.99 and a maximum iteration limit of 5, 000 . A dynamic early stopping condition based on target confidence (i.e., success probability) is used. The iteration limit is reduced as inliers are found using the RANSAC formula and the current best inlier set as an estimate of the true inlier ratio [57]. This allows MC-RANSAC to stop iterating sooner when the found inlier ratio suggests a high probability of success. The

D. Experiment 2: Real-Time Performance vs. ES-PTAM 

The second experiment evaluates the accuracy of the keytime pipeline in real time. A producer-consumer architec-ture was implemented to allow the back end to skip event clusters when necessary, an approach conceptually analogous to keyframing [3]. The real-time keytime estimator is evaluated on sequences from DSEC and MVSEC. Its global errors were better than ES-PTAM for all but one of the tested sequences while running at an average estimation frequency of 22 Hz on MVSEC and 6 Hz on DSEC (Table II). The WNOA prior ensures physically founded trajectories that have better relative errors in all tested sequences (Table III). ES-PTAM reports real-time performance on the lower-resolution MVSEC but not on the higher-resolution DSEC, but it also a provides a semidense map. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper presents a real-time, continuous-time stereo VO pipeline designed specifically for event cameras. The pipeline uses a GP and a WNOA prior to represent the trajectory at sparse keytimes to decouple state size from the number of measurements and reduce computational burden. The estimator preserves the native temporal fidelity of the sensor by evaluating reprojection errors at unique measurement timestamps by interpolating with a WNOA motion prior. The efficiency and accuracy of the pipeline is evaluated on the MVSEC and DSEC datasets. The keytime formulation reduces state size relative to GPCT without compromising estimation accuracy. It outperforms ES-PTAM in both global and relative error on all but one of the tested sequences. It achieves real-time performance on both datasets by using a producer-consumer architecture while ES-PTAM only reports real-time performance on the smaller resolution MVSEC dataset. Future work will focus on comparing different event representations and features in the pipeline. ACKNOWLEDGMENT

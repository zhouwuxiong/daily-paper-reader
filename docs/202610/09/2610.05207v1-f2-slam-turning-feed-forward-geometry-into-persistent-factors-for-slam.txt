ABSTRACT 

Feed-forward 3D models provide strong multi-view geometric priors, while on-line simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) relies mainly on local mea-surements and can accumulate drift over long sequences. Existing attempts to combine the two typically treat feed-forward predictions as an external geomet-ric state that is aligned or fused with the online estimate after the fact, which keeps broader multi-view evidence outside the optimizer that refines the SLAM state. We present F2SLAM , which instead converts feed-forward geometry di-rectly into optimization-native target-weight measurements attached to a persis-tent dense factor graph. A high-frequency stream maintains local tracking con-straints and graph connectivity, while a low-frequency stream uses wider multi-view context to selectively refresh existing measurements after a state-consistency check. Both streams constrain the same poses, inverse depths, and optional cam-era intrinsics through a single dense bundle adjustment. Experiments on multiple benchmarks demonstrate consistently strong trajectory estimation and improved dense reconstruction in both calibrated and uncalibrated settings. Notably, the uncalibrated configuration reduces the average ATE RMSE from 0.030 m for the strongest feed-forward baseline to 0.002 m on the Replica dataset. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Online visual simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) estimates camera motion and scene geometry by maintaining an optimizable geometric state that dense bundle adjustment (BA) continu-ously revisits, re-weights, and jointly refines as new observations arrive (Mur-Artal & Tard´ os, 2017; Teed & Deng, 2021; Zhao et al., 2026). Because these measurements are drawn mainly from short temporal windows, online SLAM remains sensitive to weak overlap, occlusion, viewpoint changes, and accumulated tracking error over long sequences. Recent feed-forward 3D foundation models (FMs) (Wang et al., 2025a) offer a complementary form of geometric reasoning. By jointly processing multiple images, they directly infer depth, point maps, camera poses, and multi-view structure over much broader context than a local BA window (Xu & Oishi, 2026a). This has motivated foundation-model-based SLAM (FM-SLAM) systems such as MASt3R-SLAM (Murai et al., 2025), VGGT-SLAM (Maggio et al., 2025), and VGGT-SLAM++ (Mandal et al., 2026), which exploit learned geometry for tracking, mapping, re-localization, loop closure, or submap alignment. These systems, however, typically compute feed-forward predictions over local windows or submaps and only afterwards reconcile them with the SLAM state through alignment, fusion, or state-level correction, as shown in Fig. 1. Feed-forward geometry therefore remains an external state rather than evidence that the online optimizer can act on directly. This separation is costly in two ways. Broader multi-view evidence never enters the measurement set that dense BA actually optimizes. Once a submap has been aligned, it also cannot be jointly re-weighted against subsequent local evidence, since the correction is applied only once. The missing link is a way to make feed-forward geometric evidence directly and repeatedly participate in persistent SLAM optimization, rather than serve as a one-time external correction.

We address this with F2SLAM , which follows one guiding principle: feed-forward geometry should enter SLAM as optimization-native measurements, not as a separate geometric state. F2SLAM converts multi-view feed-forward evidence into dense target-weight measurements attached directly to the existing factor graph, so that feed-forward reasoning and recurrent tracking constrain the same keyframe poses, dense inverse depths, and optional camera intrinsics through one dense BA backend. Doing so online requires updating these measurements at two different timescales. A high-frequency stream maintains local constraints and graph connectivity, while a low-frequency stream uses wider multi-view context to selectively refresh existing measurements. Because feed-forward predictions can be unreliable under occlusion or weak overlap, the central difficulty is deciding when a refresh should be trusted. F 2SLAM addresses this with a state-consistency check that admits a candidate measurement only when it agrees with the current geometric state, and otherwise leaves the existing measurement unchanged. Feed-forward pose and depth predictions may also provide gauge-aligned initialization, but subsequent geometric refinement remains entirely within the persistent BA state. In summary, our contributions are as follows: • We formulate feed-forward multi-view geometry as optimization-native target-weight mea-surements that directly participate in persistent dense BA. • We develop a dual-timescale measurement-refresh mechanism with state-consistent admis-sion, enabling local tracking and broader multi-view reasoning to update a common factor representation within a single optimized geometric state. • We instantiate this formulation in a complete online system, F 2SLAM, and demonstrate strong trajectory estimation and dense reconstruction across calibrated and uncalibrated indoor and outdoor benchmarks.

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction priors. Feed-forward reconstruction models have become strong priors for multi-view geometry. Pairwise methods such as DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024) and MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024) predict dense structure and correspondences for wide-baseline recon-struction, while multi-view models such as VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) extend this to joint camera, depth, point-map, and track prediction. Streaming variants (Zhuo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026a; Xu & Oishi, 2026b) support online or long-sequence reconstruction with memory or causal processing, but remain window-dependent and lack persistent sequence-level optimization. 

SLAM with feed-forward reconstruction priors. Recent foundation-model-based SLAM sys-tems (Liu et al., 2025; Murai et al., 2025; Maggio et al., 2025) have explored using feed-forward reconstruction priors for online tracking and mapping, often by applying them to local windows or submaps and then aligning, fusing, or correcting the resulting poses, depths, and point maps. However, the correction they provide is applied only once, since feed-forward predictions either sit outside the main dense BA or are optimized through a separate alignment stage that is not re-visited as new local evidence arrives. F 2SLAM instead converts feed-forward outputs into dense target-weight factors and jointly optimizes them with local DROID-style factors in one dense BA backend, directly coupling online tracking with feed-forward measurements predicted from broader multi-view context.

Intrinsic refinement. In the uncalibrated setting, F 2SLAM jointly optimizes camera intrinsics, poses, and inverse depths rather than fixing ground-truth calibration. The paired results in Tabs. 1 to 4 show consistently lower average ATE, suggesting that joint refinement better adapts the geo-metric state to each sequence without requiring accurate calibration in advance. Ablation of feed-forward integration. Tab. 7 evaluates feed-forward initialization, measure-ment refresh, gauge alignment, and state-consistent gating on Replica dataset. Both initializa-tion and measurement refresh independently improve performance over basic tracking. The full model achieves the strongest overall performance, reducing ATE from 0.0027 m to 0.0023 m and improving reconstruction accuracy and completion from 0.0303 m and 0.0188 m to 0.0266 m and 

0.0174 m, respectively. Removing gauge alignment degrades both trajectory and reconstruction quality, highlighting the importance of expressing predictions in the current SLAM gauge. Without state-consistent gating, performance degrades severely, demonstrating the need to reject inconsis-tent low-frequency predictions before measurement refresh. Further ablations on the low-frequency target-motion threshold, context window size, and gating threshold are provided in Appendix A.5. 

5 CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

F2SLAM follows a single principle: feed-forward geometry should enter SLAM as optimization-native measurements rather than as a separate geometric state. A high-frequency tracking stream and a low-frequency feed-forward stream convert their predictions into dense target-weight mea-surements on a persistent factor graph, allowing the same poses, inverse depths, and optional camera intrinsics to be jointly refined through a single dense BA backend with a state-consistency check. The resulting system achieves strong trajectory estimation and dense reconstruction across the eval-uated benchmarks. 9F2SLAM does not currently include explicit loop closure, which limits performance on long-horizon outdoor sequences such as KITTI Odometry. Performance also depends on feed-forward prediction reliability, which can degrade under weak texture, dynamic objects, large viewpoint changes, and scale ambiguity. Incorporating loop closure and improving the robustness of low-frequency mea-surements are natural directions for future work.

Importantly, performance does not noticeably degrade, indicating that the candidate gating mechanism prevents unreliable low-frequency measurements from strongly perturbing the active graph. We therefore adopt 800 as the default target motion. 

Window size. Fig. 4 evaluates maximum context sizes from 4 to 40 keyframes while keeping the data, optimizer, trigger threshold, and gating rules fixed. The two smallest settings produce catastrophic failures because their temporal support is insufficient to establish reliable multi-view geometry. With only a few keyframes, the window provides limited baseline and viewpoint diver-sity, making correspondence and depth predictions poorly constrained. The resulting low-frequency measurements are therefore noisy and can be rejected inconsistently by the state-consistency gate. This effect is especially severe in the smallest windows, where a small change in the sampled views can substantially alter the available geometric evidence. Once the window reaches a moderate size ( 12 keyframes and above), it contains enough overlap-ping yet non-redundant views to support stable correspondence, depth, and confidence estimation. Candidate gating then suppresses residual outliers before they refresh graph factors, which explains the sharp recovery and the relatively flat, non-monotonic curves in the remaining range. Adding more keyframes beyond this regime mostly introduces redundant observations and does not yield a consistent geometric gain. We therefore use 20 keyframes as the default context size: it attains the best Accuracy while remaining close to the best completion and within the stable ATE regime. This is a balanced choice across the three metrics rather than a claim of uniformly optimal performance. Overall, these ablations show that F 2SLAM requires a minimum amount of low-frequency temporal support, after which performance is relatively insensitive to the exact window size. This robustness is enabled by candidate gating and measurement-level factor refresh, which allow measurements conditioned on broader multi-view context to update the graph while suppressing unreliable updates.

Abstract: This paper introduces Hybrid DeepSEE (HDS), a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) neuro-symbolic framework for proactive drift anticipation in Visual SLAM (V-SLAM). While data-driven models offer predictive power, their “black-box” nature often yields physically in-consistent outputs in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. To address this, HDS integrates neural drift risk estimation with symbolic constraint reasoning. By utilizing a Large Language Model (LLM) as a reasoning bridge, the framework translates qualitative human context into interpretable symbolic constraints. Building upon this architecture, we propose a superior drift anticipation framework that ensures enhanced reliability and consistency in Visual SLAM 1. INTRODUCTION Reliable state estimation is a fundamental requirement for the safe deployment of autonomous mobile robots in mission-critical domains such as industrial automation and defense operations. In such systems, localization ac-curacy directly affects feedback control performance, tra-jectory tracking stability, and overall operational safety (Kelly and Sukhatme, 2009). Visual Simultaneous Local-ization and Mapping (V-SLAM) has become a widely adopted solution due to its ability to extract rich envi-ronmental information using low-cost cameras (Cadena et al., 2016). Real-world V-SLAM systems, despite their advantages, experience cumulative drift, which refers to the accumu-lated errors in pose estimation while driving (Cadena et al., 2016). As a result, there is a divergence between the estimated and true trajectories. From a control perspec-tive, the estimation degradation can propagate through the feedback loop, leading to performance deterioration or potential instability (Radisavljevic-Gajic, 2024). Drift is particularly pronounced in challenging environments characterized by repetitive textures, abrupt illumination changes, and low-feature regions, where measurement re-liability is significantly reduced (Savinykh et al., 2022). In safety-critical domains, such as nuclear maintenance, defense operations, and human-robot collaboration, es-timation failures can lead to catastrophic consequences including human casualties or severe environmental dam-age. In these high-stakes contexts, even minor SLAM drift transcends simple positioning errors, potentially trig-gering abrupt trajectory deviations that propagate into the control loop (Cadena et al., 2016). Therefore, it is essential not only to correct errors after they occur but to anticipate degradation before significant drift compro-mises the system. Early awareness of drift risk enables proactive supervisory actions—such as motion regulation

2.1 Geometric Visual SLAM 

Classical visual SLAM systems rely on geometric consis-tency, feature matching, and nonlinear optimization to estimate camera trajectories. Representative systems such as ORB-SLAM3 (Campos et al., 2021) utilized feature-based tracking, local bundle adjustment, and loop clo-sure detection to refine pose estimates. These approaches achieve high accuracy in structured and well-textured environments through principled optimization over pose graphs. However, geometric SLAM systems are fundamentally re-active: accumulated drift is corrected only after sufficient loop evidence is detected. In large-scale exploration or non-revisitable trajectories, loop closure cannot be guar-arXiv:2610.05757v1 [cs.RO] 5 Oct 2026 Fig. 1. Furthermore, feature-based methods degrade significantly in challeng-ing environments—such as low-texture regions or areas with illumination volatility—where reliable feature corre-spondences become sparse or ambiguous. Conventional mitigation strategies primarily rely on loop closure detection, which compensates accumulated er-rors after revisiting previously mapped locations (Ho and Newman, 2006). Although effective in structured environ-ments, loop closure mechanisms are inherently reactive and provide no guarantee of bounded estimation perfor-mance during large-scale exploration or high-speed ma-neuvers (Cadena et al., 2016). In safety-critical systems, it is insufficient to only rely on the traditional loop closure mechanisms due to safety. 

2.2 Learning-Based Drift and Error Estimation 

Recent advances have incorporated deep learning into SLAM pipelines to improve robustness and error aware-ness. In particular, SEESys–the state-of-the-art online extension of the DeepSEE(Hu et al., 2023) framework– introduced an edge-assisted architecture that achieves real-time pose error estimation with an average end-to-end latency of 37.3 ms (Hu et al., 2024). By leveraging an attention-based multi-modality model (TimeSformer), SEESys demonstrates the feasibility of delivering instan-taneous quality-of-service (QoS) metrics for mobile robots and AR devices. However, despite their real-time capabilities, recent ap-proaches that integrate deep neural networks (DNNs) into V-SLAM pipelines to enhance perceptual robust-ness remain fundamentally data-driven black-box sys-tems. While these methods can produce numerically plau-sible motion estimates, they lack mechanisms to formally enforce geometric or kinematic consistency. As a result, physically inadmissible trajectories may emerge, particu-larly in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, where such inconsistencies can arise unpredictably and intro-duce additional risk into downstream control processes (Radisavljevic-Gajic, 2024). Furthermore, although sys-tems such as SEESys provide auditory feedback to human operators, they do not offer explainable reasoning nor enable dynamic injection of contextual constraints into the inference loop.

2.3 Neuro-Symbolic and Constraint-Aware Learning 

Neuro-symbolic learning aims to integrate data-driven perception with symbolic reasoning to overcome the lim-itations of black-box models. Logical Neural Networks (LNNs) enable differentiable reasoning under explicitly defined logical rules, offering improved interpretability and consistency. However, their application to proactive drift anticipation in robotic localization remains largely unexplored. In contrast to prior work, our framework integrates neural drift risk estimation with symbolic constraint enforcement under a Human-in-the-Loop paradigm. By leveraging a Large Language Model (LLM) as a reasoning bridge to encode expert-defined constraints, we enable a Hybrid DeepSEE (HDS) approach that aligns learned represen-tations with formal safety and kinematic principles. 3. 3.5 Supervisory Drift Monitoring and Decision Support 

The final anticipation score ˜ rt is provided to a super-visory monitoring layer that operates independently of the underlying SLAM estimator. Importantly, the pro-posed framework does not modify or correct the estimated pose ˆPt; instead, it evaluates localization reliability in real time and provides decision-level guidance. When the anticipated drift risk exceeds a predefined threshold, the supervisory layer may trigger high-level safety actions such as: 

• Motion scaling or velocity reduction, 

• Re-localization request, 

• Human operator notification. It is important to note that the LLM’s reasoning process is executed asynchronously only when significant envi-ronmental changes are detected or explicit user inputs are provided, ensuring that high-level symbolic reasoning does not compromise the real-time constraints of the underlying V-SLAM backend. 

3.6 Quantitative Evaluation and Experimental Results 

To validate the performance of the proposed Hybrid DeepSEE (HDS) framework, we conducted comparative experiments using the EuRoC MAV dataset, specifically the challenging MH 04 difficult sequence. We evaluated the proactive drift anticipation capability of HDS against both a standard Random Forest baseline and DeepSEE (Hu et al., 2023). The experimental results indicate that HDS generally provides longer Early Warning Lead Times compared to conventional reactive approaches. Across major drift events (e.g., E39 to E327), HDS predicted significant tracking degradation an average of 6.2 seconds in advance, whereas DeepSEE achieved an average lead time of 2.4 seconds or failed to anticipate certain drift events entirely. Furthermore, we evaluated the Early Warning Rate (EWR) under varying qualities of language-mediated hu-man annotations. Under robust expert-provided contex-tual annotations (“good annot”), HDS achieved a peak EWR of 91.5% for a ≥1s lead-time window and main-tained a relatively high reliability of 85.4% even under the stricter ≥5s condition. In contrast, DeepSEE without contextual assistance showed a noticeable performance decline, dropping to 78.5% at the ≥5s threshold. However, the experiments also revealed that the overall performance of HDS is highly dependent on the qual-ity and consistency of human-provided language inputs. Accurate and context-rich annotations from expert users significantly improved prediction reliability and antici-pation performance, whereas ambiguous or low-quality annotations could reduce system effectiveness. This ob-servation suggests that the proposed framework strongly relies on Human-in-the-Loop interaction, where the qual-ity of linguistic context directly influences the final safety prediction outcome. These results demonstrate that language-mediated sym-bolic constraints can help compensate for neural uncer-tainty and support more reliable and proactive safety regulation in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. 4. CONCLUSION This paper presents Hybrid DeepSEE (HDS), a human-in-the-loop neuro-symbolic framework designed to proac-tively anticipate drift errors in Visual SLAM. By fus-ing data-driven neural perception with language-mediated symbolic reasoning, the architecture effectively addresses the ”black-box” limitations and physical inconsistencies prevalent in conventional deep learning models. The primary structural contribution of this work lies in decoupling drift-risk anticipation from the underly-ing state-estimation process, establishing a robust su-pervisory safety layer that preserves the integrity of the SLAM backend. Crucially, empirical evaluations demon-strate that integrating expert-defined geometric and kine-matic constraints via a language interface significantly extends the early warning lead time and enhances pre-diction reliability. This framework shifts drift monitoring from a reactive recovery process to a proactive, explain-able, and trustworthy component of autonomous robotic navigation. 5. FUTURE WORKS This research presents a conceptual framework, so we outline the following future research plans: (1) Adaptive Weight Optimization Strategy: We will develop an algorithm to determine the optimal weighting coefficients (wn, λ k, γ j ) for fusing neural risk, symbolic rules, and hardware constraints. This includes applying adaptive scheduling techniques to increase guardrail weights once the robot approaches physical safety limits, ensuring de-terministic safety in high-risk scenarios. (2) Validation with physical humanoid: The proposed HDS frame-work will be deployed onto a humanoid for real-world testing. We will evaluate the framework’s ability to proac-tively anticipate drift and trigger supervisory interven-tions within the real-time constraints of the hardware.
